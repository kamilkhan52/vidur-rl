## Findings

### [major] M1: at K = 8 the rule cannot take the overrides that gave the pilot its gain, so a B2 "fail" is not interpretable
`headroom.py:254-271` (pre-registration), `:497-505`.

**The bar.** Overriding needs mean(d) > t_crit * sd(d)/sqrt(K), i.e. a standardized gain mean/sd > 3.238/sqrt(8) = **1.145**. Power by standardized gain: 0.28 gives 3.5 %, 0.5 gives 9 %, 1.0 gives 42 %, 1.5 gives 80 %, 2.0 gives 97 %. In the decision we saw (mean gain ~700, paired SD 2,000-3,000), the LCB is -1,590 to -2,735. Detecting that gain needs K ≈ 49-110.

**What the pilot's gain was made of.** The true-future overrides in the 4 pilot closed logs:

| Window | Overrides | Median true-future gain | q90 | Median as % of pi_e's score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| conv_w0 | 43 / 1,626 | 39 | 964 | 0.17 % |
| conv_w2000 | 29 / 1,610 | 38 | 635 | 0.16 % |
| tool_w0 | 42 / 1,658 | 34 | 463 | 0.18 % |

tool_w2000 is left out because its tails weight puts it in other units. For B2 to take such an override with even 50 % power at K = 8, the paired per-future SD must be at most gain/1.145: about 30-35 at the median and 400-840 at q90. That is one to two orders of magnitude below the one SD we saw.

**What this means.** Unless CRN pairing makes typical paired SDs that small, B2 overrides only a different class of decision: the ones where the sampled-length model is very confident. The pilot says pi_e was the true-future best in 97-98 % of decisions. The docstring's own number says 3-4 % of B decisions already passed a Bonferroni t-test at K = 4, whose bar is 2.57 SD. So B2's override rate at K = 8 is likely above the pilot's 2-3 %, on decisions picked for the model's confidence rather than for the truth. The LCB controls only the Monte Carlo error of the K-future mean. It does not control bias in the sampled-length model (the pool is outside the window, and in-flight lengths are drawn conditional on generated tokens).

**Failure scenario.** B2 runs ~900 CPU-h and fails the gate. Three explanations remain: (a) the rule is too strict for the pilot's small-gain mechanism, (b) the overrides it does take are model-confident but wrong, or (c) a planner with sampled futures cannot help. The pre-registration does not say which one a fail supports, and the data B2 logs cannot separate (a) from (b).

**Fix** (before launch; cheap):
1. Replay `select_candidate_lcb` offline on B's K = 4 logs, which hold `score_k` for every candidate. Report the override rate at K = 4 and the distribution of per-decision standardized gains. From those, predict B2's override rate at K = 8 with the noncentral t, for the pre-registration.
2. Make B2 self-diagnosing: at each decision, also run one true-future rollout per candidate (+8 rollouts, +12.5 % cost), logged and never used to decide. Whatever the gate says, B2 then measures how precise its overrides are against the truth. This separates (a), (b) and (c). Put it in the `closed_crn` sha now, before any B2 row exists.
3. Pre-register the interpretation: what a fail means at a low override rate (for example < 1 %: "underpowered for the pilot's mechanism") versus at a high one.

If (2) is not wanted, at least do (1) and (3).

### [major] M2: the K = 8 t-bound is anti-conservative for left-skewed paired gains, the case a tail score is built to catch
`headroom.py:497-505`.

**Why skew matters here.** The tail score adds w x hinge, with w ~1,774-2,336 s per unit of hinge. An alternative that is slightly better in most sampled futures but blows up a queue when a long output lands gives d with a rare, large negative value. The t-based bound assumes the sample sd reflects that spread. With 8 draws, an event of probability p is missed with probability (1-p)^8: 0.66 at p = 0.05 and 0.43 at p = 0.10. When it is missed, the sd is small and the LCB is positive, whatever the true mean is.

**Monte Carlo** (true mean gain 0; nominal per-alternative false-override rate 0.05/7 = 0.0071):

| Paired-difference distribution | False-override rate |
|---|---|
| Normal | 0.0073 |
| Left-skewed lognormal, sigma 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 | 0.036 / 0.107 / 0.225 |
| Right-skewed lognormal | ≤ 0.001 |
| Rare-loss mixture (+1 usually, a loss with p = 0.05 / 0.10 / 0.20) | 0.66 / 0.43 / 0.17 |

The rare-loss rates stay the same (0.66 at p = 0.05, 0.43 at p = 0.10) when the loss is 2-5 times larger, so that the true mean gain is clearly negative (-0.9 to -3.8).

**Failure scenario.** B2 replaces B's winner's curse (picking noise) with a systematic preference for overrides that carry tail risk. Those concentrate harm in the P99 that the gate measures. The gate and placebo still protect against a false pass, so this is about whether B2 can succeed and what a fail means, not about the validity of the gate.

**Fix:**
- Before launch, measure the skew of d on B's K = 4 logs, among the alternatives with a positive mean d. One measure: the fraction whose minimum d is below -3 x the median |d|.
- If skew is common, pre-register a remedy. One option is a larger K: K = 16 drops the bar to 0.69 SD and the miss probability at p = 0.1 to 0.19, at twice the cost. Another is a guard that does not rely on normality, for example also requiring min_j d_j > -c x |mean d|. At the least, state the limitation in the pre-registration.
- M1's true-future diagnostic would show directly whether B2's overrides go wrong in the tail.

### [minor] 1: the API default log header ignores the rule, so a mean log can be replayed as lcb
`headroom.py:1045`, `:1064`. With `header=None` (tests, ad hoc scripts), both rules write the header `{"horizon", "n_futures"}`. `closed_loop_crn(rule="lcb")` on an existing mean-rule log replays the mean rule's `chosen` silently, and vice versa. Replay never checks the rule. The CLI is safe, because `header=params` carries `deviation_rule`.

**Fix:** add `"deviation_rule": {"rule": rule, "alpha": alpha}` to the default header when `rule != "mean"`. On replay, also check `("t_crit" in row) == (rule == "lcb")`. The second check is in `closed_crn`'s sha, so do it before any B2 run.

### [minor] 2: the gate does not pin alpha to the pre-registered 0.05
`headroom.py:1817`, `:1932`, `:2129`. `b_head_rows` filters on `_rule_of(p)` only. The gate only checks that the 4 windows agree, and the gate CLI does not pass `--lcb-alpha`. Four windows run at `--lcb-alpha 0.2` would therefore pass as "B2". The alpha is visible in `windows[*].deviation_rule`, but the verdict does not enforce it.

**Fix:** give `b_head_rows`/`gate_verdict` the full rule dict (`{"rule": "lcb", "alpha": a.lcb_alpha}`) and match `p.get("deviation_rule")` exactly for lcb. The gate path in `main` then passes `a.lcb_alpha`. This is `gate_sha` only, so no row is re-keyed.

### [minor] 3: `--lcb-alpha` is silently ignored under the mean rule
`headroom.py:2103`, `:2141`, `:2215`. If the operator forgets `--deviation-rule lcb` but passes `--n-futures 8 --lcb-alpha 0.05` into a `b42_*` dir, the run is step B's mean rule at K = 8. Its params record no rule, and nothing warns. It is noticed only when placebo with `--deviation-rule lcb` finds no head, after ~220 CPU-h.

**Fix:** default `--lcb-alpha` to None, and refuse it unless the rule is lcb (use 0.05 when lcb is given without it).

### [minor] 4: the Alpine resource request conflicts with the module's own limit and is unverified
`headroom.py:184` vs `:269-271`. The module's Alpine paragraph says "acpu, <= 22 CPUs". B2 asks `--cpus-per-task=24` per chain, which is 96 CPUs for 4 chains, alongside B's remaining chain and the A searches. If a per-user or per-job QoS cap near 22 applies, the jobs sit pending (QOSMaxCpuPerUserLimit) or the chains serialize, and the "15-20 h per window" doubles. `--mem=64G` for 16 forked children is ~3.8 GB each, against B's 48G for 8 (~5.3 GB). Peak RSS per child is not stated.

**Fix:** before submitting, check `sacctmgr show qos cpu-normal` (or the current acpu limits) and one B job's MaxRSS (`sacct -j <id> -o MaxRSS`). Then either size the jobs to fit, or state the expected wall time under the cap.
